2. Market consequences

If rules arrive but data stays company-level

Compliance on paperCompany totals pass audits but can't show which site drains which basin.
Blanket risk discountsWhole countries priced as "water risk"; careful mines get no credit.
Questionnaire fatigueEvery OEM surveys the same mines. Cost multiplies, quality doesn't.
Surprise supply shocksWater permits and conflicts stop mines without warning to buyers.
Copper left outBattery due diligence covers Co, Li, Ni and graphite. Copper sites stay dark.

3. Desired future

Different from the "nothing changes" path

A passport per siteWithdrawal, consumption and discharge kept separate, with source quality and basin stress.
Every number says how it was madeMeasured, calculated or estimated, graded A–E.
Gaps filled, then replacedOpen ML estimates until metered data arrives.
One set of numbersBuyers, banks, researchers, communities read the same data.
Water in the contractOfftake and finance terms reward water-responsible sites.

Who pays, the pilot, and what is proven

Mines fill the passport for free. Buyers, lenders and traders pay to rely on it.

Pays: EU battery & cell makers, OEMs

Due-diligence duty from Aug 2027. One passport replaces dozens of supplier questionnaires.

Pays: lenders & traders

Site-level water grade for project finance and offtake screening.

Free: mines & researchers

One format for every buyer; open site data for research groups like WU Wien to train and validate models.
