Beyond reporting · reconcile → predict → decide

What does a missing litre of water cost each mine?

Parsing a report gives you numbers. This engine tells you which number to trust, how likely each mine is to run short of freshwater by 2030, how much revenue that puts at risk, and which fix pays back first.

Site
1

Reconcile

Fuses three independent views of each mine's water: the company's figure, what its own water balance implies, and the WU Wien satellite + ML model. Each gets an error bar; the engine returns one best estimate and a trust score.

2

Predict

1,000 simulated futures per mine to 2030: demand growth, droughts, tighter licences. Output: probability of a freshwater shortfall and revenue at risk, in dollars.

3

Decide

Prices each fix (use less freshwater, secure a new source) against the risk it removes. The mine sees payback; the buyer sees supply risk; the bank sees a number it can lend against.

Why a spreadsheet can't copy this. Every report we ingest becomes a reconciled site ledger that recalibrates the model for mines that don't report. The more companies use it, the narrower everyone's risk band gets. The asset is the reconciled dataset and the error model behind it, not the parser.

Each source gets an error bar: reported ±10% (±3% once the site balance closes), balance-implied ±10%, model ±35% (R² 0.79 on test data). The engine weights each source by its precision and flags sources that disagree.
